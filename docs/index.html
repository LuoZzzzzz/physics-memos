Physics Memos

I think much of undergraduate education is about asking and answering your own questions. Unfortunately, my brain seems to be highly efficient at deleting the insights gained from this process. So after two years of university I have decided to write my notes on a blog. I think there's two good reasons for this. First, I will remember what I wrote here. Second, because this is on the web and in principle anyone can read it (but does anyone actually? :3), I have to write correct and logically consistent things to avoid embarassing myself. This promotes clear and honest thinking.

Being a Physics novice some of the things I write here may be straight up wrong, and I try to indicate passages and ideas that I find suspicious. If anyone happens to bump into this page, use the information here at your own risk!

Physics as a Hobby

After 3 years, I have now graduated from Oxford with BA in Physics. Although I am no longer pursuing a career in Physics research (having since defected to finance), I still find Physics to be a fascinating hobby. I will continue to write notes on Physics topics that I find interesting, and I hope to keep this blog alive for many years to come.

Lie Groups

So much of modern Physics is built on the ideas of symmetry, and a large part of that idea is captured through the mathematics of Lie groups. Unfortunately, this topic was never covered much in my undergraduate courses (at least not in the BA), and it is therefore the first thing that I want to learn about in my post-undergraduate Physics journey. The notes here will be largely based off the book "Lie Algebras in Particle Physics" by Howard Georgi, which I found to be quite similar in content to the B4 Particle Physics course at Oxford.
