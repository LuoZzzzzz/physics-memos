    Einstein solid

    A crystal in $3$ dimensions with $N$ oscillators will have a total of $3N$ vibrational modes, as each oscillator has $3$ vibrational degrees of freedom. We treat the oscillators as independent harmonic oscillators, each with a fixed natural frequency $\omega_0$.

    The density of states in $\omega$ space is simply: $$g(\omega) = 3N \delta(\omega - \omega_0)$$

    The occupancy of each oscillator is: $$n_\text{o}(\omega) = \frac{1}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega}-1}$$ Therefore, the total number of phonons at a given temperature is: $$N_\text{p} = 3N\int_0^\infty \frac{\delta(\omega-\omega_0)}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega}-1} \text{d}\omega = \frac{3N}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega_0}-1}$$ While the internal energy of the crystal is: $$U(T) = \frac{3N \hbar\omega_0}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega_0}-1}$$ since all phonons have the same energy $\hbar \omega_0$.

    Differentiating gives: $$C_V = \frac{\partial U}{\partial T} = 3N \hbar\omega_0 \frac{\hbar\omega_0}{k_B T^2}\frac{e^{\beta\hbar\omega_0}}{(e^{\beta\hbar\omega_0}-1)^2} = 3Nk_B\left(\frac{\Theta_E}{T}\right)^2 \frac{e^{\Theta_E/T}}{(e^{\Theta_E/T}-1)^2}$$ where we have defined the Einstein temperature $\Theta_E = \hbar\omega_0/k_B$.

    At high temperature, $C_V \rightarrow 3Nk_B$, which is expected.

    At low temperature, $C_V \rightarrow 3Nk_B\left(\frac{\Theta_E}{T}\right)^2 e^{-\Theta_D/T}$, which means that the heat capacity is exponentially suppressed. However, this disagrees with experiments which suggests a $T^3$ dependence.

    Debye solid

    A crystal in $3$ dimensions with $N$ oscillators will have a total of $N$ unique $\mathbf{k}$ states. Each $\mathbf{k}$ has a further degeneracy of $3$ as it is able to support $2$ transverse and $1$ longitudinal mode.

    Let us further assume that:
    1. The dispersion relation (between $\omega$ and $k$) has the form $\omega = vk$ where $v$ is the sound speed (group velocity at long wavelengths).
    2. Density of states $g(k) \text{d}k \propto k^2$.
    3. Finite cutoff frequency $\omega_D$.

    The density of states, accounting for the $3$ degeneracies per $\mathbf{k}$ mode, is: $$ 3\sum_\mathbf{k} \rightarrow 3\frac{V}{(2\pi)^3}\int \text{d}^3 \mathbf{x} = 3\frac{4\pi V}{(2\pi)^3} \int_0^\infty k^2 \text{d}k \implies g(k) \text{d}k = 3\frac{4\pi V}{(2\pi)^3} k^2 \text{d}k$$ Applying the dispersion relation gives: $$g(\omega) \text{d}\omega = 3\frac{4\pi V}{(2\pi v)^3} \omega^2 \text{d}\omega$$

    Debye frequency, $\omega_D$
    The cutoff frequency is defined such that integration up to it gives exactly $3N$ modes, which is the total number of unique modes the crystal has. Therefore: $$ \int_0^{\omega_D} g(\omega) \text{d}\omega = \frac{4\pi V}{(2\pi v)^3} \omega_D^3 = 3N \implies \boxed{\omega_D = (6 \pi^2 n)^{1/3} v} $$

    Statistical mechanics
    Each mode of the crystal behaves like a harmonic oscillator, which has occupancy: $$\boxed{n_\text{o}(\omega) = \frac{1}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega}-1}}$$ Therefore, the number of phonons in the crystal is: $$N_\text{p} = \int_0^{\omega_D} n_\text{o}(\omega) g(\omega) \text{d}\omega = 3\frac{4\pi V}{(2\pi v)^3}\int_0^{\omega_D} \frac{\omega^2}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega}-1} \text{d}\omega$$ while the total energy of the crystal, as a function of temperature is: $$U(T) = \int_0^{\omega_D} \hbar \omega \,n_\text{o}(\omega) g(\omega) \text{d}\omega = 3\frac{4\pi V \hbar}{(2\pi v)^3}\int_0^{\omega_D} \frac{\omega^3}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega}-1} \text{d}\omega$$

    Low temperature approximation
    At low $T$, $\beta \rightarrow \infty$, so $\frac{1}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega}-1}$ term decreases quickly and essentially kills off anything for large $\omega \lt \omega_D$. Therefore, we may replace the limits of integration: $$\int_0^{\omega_D} \rightarrow \int_0^\infty$$ This reduces the integrals for $N_\text{p}$ and $U(T)$ to standard Zeta integrals. We expect that for low temperature the heat capacity $C_V = \tfrac{\partial U}{\partial T}$ scales as $T^3$.

    High temperature approximation
    At high $T$, $\beta \rightarrow 0$, so $e^{\beta \hbar \omega} - 1 \approx 1 + \beta\hbar\omega - 1 = \beta\hbar\omega$, so the occupancy becomes: $$n_\text{o}(\omega) = \frac{1}{e^{\beta \hbar \omega}-1} \approx \frac{1}{\beta\hbar\omega}$$ which reduces the integrals for $N_\text{p}$ and $U(T)$ to simple polynomial integrals. We expect that $C_V = \tfrac{\partial U}{\partial T} \rightarrow 3Nk_B$.
