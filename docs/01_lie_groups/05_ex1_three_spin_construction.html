Full Construction on a Three-Spin Hilbert Space

10 October 2026
Digitised from handwritten notes.

Contents

    Set-up: three spin-$\tfrac12$ particles

    The space of three spin-$\h$ particles decomposes as $$\begin{aligned}\mathcal H_1\otimes\mathcal H_2\otimes\mathcal H_3 &= \h\otimes\h\otimes\h = \big(\h\otimes\h\big)\otimes\h \\ &= \big(\underbrace{0\oplus1}_{j_{12}=0,\,1}\big)\otimes\h = \big(0\otimes\h\big)\oplus\big(1\otimes\h\big) \\ &= \underbrace{\h}_{\substack{j_{12}=0\\ \text{label }\alpha=1}} \;\oplus\; \underbrace{\h}_{\substack{j_{12}=1\\ \alpha=2}} \;\oplus\; \underbrace{\tth}_{j_{12}=1}.\end{aligned}$$ The eight states $\ka{m}{1}$, $\ka{m}{2}$, $\ket{\tth,m}$ form the original basis. Spin $\h$ occurs twice, and $\alpha$ tells the two copies apart.

    A spin-1 tensor operator

    The spin operators on the full space are $$J_i = S_i^{(1)} + S_i^{(2)} + S_i^{(3)},$$ where $S_i^{(k)}$ acts on the $k$-th factor and as the identity on the others. Take the operator $O_i$ to be $S_i^{(2)}$. It is a tensor operator because $$\big[J_a, S_b^{(2)}\big] = i\varepsilon_{abc}\,S_c^{(2)} = \big[J_a^{\text{adj},\,s=1}\big]_{cb}\,S_c^{(2)}, \qquad \big[J_a^{\text{adj}}\big]_{cb} = -i\varepsilon_{acb}.$$ This means that $$\Big\{\, \Sl{\pm1} = \mp\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(S_1^{(2)}\pm iS_2^{(2)}\big),\quad \Sl{0}=S_3^{(2)} \Big\}$$ form a spin-1 tensor operator in this space.

    Conventions
    • $J_\pm = J_1\pm iJ_2$, so $J_\pm\ket{j,m} = C^\pm(j,m)\,\ket{j,m\pm1}$ with $C^\pm(j,m) = \sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}$.
    • For the tensor operator, $\big[J_\pm,\Sl{l}\big] = C^\pm(1,l)\,\Sl{l\pm1}$.
    • Single-particle ladder operators carry the same normalisation as $J_\pm$: $S^{(2)}_\pm = S^{(2)}_1\pm iS^{(2)}_2$, with $S_+\ket{\D}=\ket{\U}$ and $S_-\ket{\U}=\ket{\D}$. Hence $$\Sl{\pm1} = \mp\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,S^{(2)}_\pm ,$$ an additional signed factor of $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$ compared with $J_\pm$.
    • Clebsch–Gordan coefficients are written $\CG{J,M}{s,l;\,j,m}$, with the operator's spin $s$ first, in the Condon–Shortley convention.

    General construction: two routes

    Take one of the irreps: $(\h,\alpha{=}1)$, $(\h,\alpha{=}2)$, or $(\tth)$.

    Route 1: product states. Apply $\Sl{\pm1,0}$ to all states of the irrep. For example, taking the $(\h,\alpha{=}2)$ irrep generates 6 states: $$\begin{gathered}\Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2},\quad \Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2},\quad \Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2},\\ \Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2},\quad \Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2},\quad \Sl{-1}\ka{\mh}{2}.\end{gathered}$$ These are the “product” states.

    What the “isomorphism” carries over

    $\Sl{l}\ket{\h,m}$ is “isomorphic” to $\ket{1,l}\ket{\h,m}$: the correspondence $\ket{1,l}\ket{\h,m}\mapsto\Sl{l}\ka{m}{2}$ is linear and commutes with the $J_a$ (that is the content of the tensor-operator commutator). Every linear relation among the $\ket{1,l}\ket{\h,m}$ therefore carries over to the product states, including every Clebsch–Gordan expansion and every result of acting with $J_\pm$.

    Inner products do not carry over. The $\ket{1,l}\ket{\h,m}$ are orthonormal, so the 6 product states are orthonormal only symbolically, as abstract labels. As vectors in $\mathcal H_1\otimes\mathcal H_2\otimes\mathcal H_3$ they need not be normalised or orthogonal, and some may even vanish. The only orthogonality guaranteed in the actual space comes from $J_3$ and $J^2$: states with different eigenvalues are orthogonal.

    Route 2: highest-weight construction. Here we use $1\otimes\h=\h\oplus\tth$, read as (tensor operator) $\otimes$ (irrep).

    • Start with $$\tk{\tth}{\tth} = \Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2}.$$
    • Lower both sides.

    LHS: $J_-\tk{\tth}{\tth} = C^-(\tth,\tth)\,\tk{\tth}{\h} = \sqrt3\,\tk{\tth}{\h}$.

    RHS: $J_-\big(\Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2}\big)$. Again there are two routes:

    1. work it out explicitly using the commutator;
    2. understand that (a) implies $\Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2}\simeq\ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\h}$, and so just use angular-momentum addition.

    Let us use (a): $$\begin{aligned}J_-\Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2} &= \big[J_-,\Sl{+1}\big]\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{+1}J_-\ka{\h}{2}\\ &= C^-(1,1)\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{+1}\,C^-(\h,\h)\ka{\mh}{2}\\ &= \sqrt2\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}.\end{aligned}$$ So $$\tk{\tth}{\h} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}.$$

    Now we want to move “laterally” to the $\tk{\h}{\h}$ state. Both $\tk{\tth}{\h}$ and $\tk{\h}{\h}$ must be linear combinations of $\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}$ and $\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2}$, the only product states with $M=\h$. Requiring $\tk{\h}{\h}$ to be symbolically orthogonal to $\tk{\tth}{\h}$, with the Condon–Shortley sign (the coefficient of the $l=+1$ term positive), gives $$\tk{\h}{\h} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}.$$ We can then continue lowering each ladder to find all the states.

    Comparing the routes. Route 1 produces $$\begin{array}{c|ccc} & \Sl{-1} & \Sl{0} & \Sl{+1}\\ \hline \ka{\h}{2} & \Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} & \Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} & \Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2}\\ \ka{\mh}{2} & \Sl{-1}\ka{\mh}{2} & \Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2} & \Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2} \end{array}$$ while Route 2 produces $$\begin{array}{c} \tk{\h}{\h}\\[6pt] \tk{\h}{\mh} \end{array} \;\oplus\; \begin{array}{c} \tk{\tth}{\tth}\\[6pt] \tk{\tth}{\h}\\[6pt] \tk{\tth}{\mh}\\[6pt] \tk{\tth}{\mtth} \end{array}$$ The two sets are related by the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, just as the states $\ket{J,M;\,s,j}$ and $\ket{s,l}\ket{j,m}$ are related.

    Where these states live

    Whichever route we take, the 6 states live in $\mathcal H_1\otimes\mathcal H_2\otimes\mathcal H_3$. They can therefore be expressed in terms of the single-spin states $\{\ket{\U\U\U},\ket{\U\U\D},\dots\}$, or in terms of the states $\{\ket{\tth,\h},\ka{\h}{1},\dots\}$ of the original decomposition $\h\oplus\h\oplus\tth$.

    For example, the $\tk{\h}{\h}$ state must be made up of the $\ka{\h}{1}$ and $\ka{\h}{2}$ states, because these transform the same way under the $J_a$. So $$\tk{\h}{\h} = k_{\alpha=2,\beta=1}\,\kb{\h}{1} + k_{\alpha=2,\beta=2}\,\kb{\h}{2},$$ where $\beta$ labels the copy of spin $\h$ in the original basis that the state lands in. To find $\tk{\h}{\h}$ explicitly and evaluate the action of the $\Sl{l}$ (that is, to find the $k_{\alpha\beta}$), we need to expand on the $\ket{\U\D\U}$ etc. states. We do this explicitly in the highest-weight construction below.

    Further notes on the structure

    $\Sl{\pm1,0}$ acting on

    • $(\h,\alpha{=}1)$ produces 6 states,
    • $(\h,\alpha{=}2)$ produces 6 states,
    • $(\tth)$ produces 12 states.

    Each set of 6, 6 and 12 states is orthonormal only symbolically. In the actual space none of the three sets is orthonormal in general, and states from different sets (for instance the first 6 and the second 6) are in general not orthogonal to one another either. What does hold: within one set, the Route-2 states with different $(J,M)$ are orthogonal or zero, since they have different eigenvalues of the Hermitian operators $J^2$ and $J_3$.

    Twelve states in an eight-dimensional space

    $\Sl{l}$ acting on the $(\tth)$ irrep gives 12 states, but the original space is only 8-dimensional!

    Resolution

    The 12 states are orthonormal only symbolically, so there is no contradiction: 12 vectors in an 8-dimensional space simply have to be linearly dependent. In Route 2 the 12 states organise as $1\otimes\tth=\h\oplus\tth\oplus\fh$, that is, $2+4+6$ states. The entire $\widetilde{J}=\fh$ tower (6 of the 12 states) is zero, because the original space contains no spin $\fh$ for it to land in (shown below). The remaining $\widetilde{J}=\tth$ and $\widetilde{J}=\h$ towers are nonzero.

    In Route 1 only two of the product states vanish, $\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\tth}$ and $\Sl{-1}\ket{\tth,\mtth}$, but the 12 product states span only a 6-dimensional subspace.

    Now we work through some calculations explicitly.

    The $\lvert\widetilde{5/2,m}\rangle$ states are zero

    Start from the top state: $$\tq{\fh}{\fh} = \Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\tth}.$$ We know that $\ket{\tth,\tth}=\ket{\U\U\U}$ and that $\Sl{+1} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}S^{(2)}_+ = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(S^{(2)}_1+iS^{(2)}_2\big)$. So $$\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\tth} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,S^{(2)}_+\ket{\U\U\U} = 0 .$$ The top state $\tq{\fh}{\fh}$ is therefore already $0$. Any lower state, such as $\tq{\fh}{\tth}$, is obtained by acting on it with $J_-$, which means they are all $0$ too.

    Finding the adjacent tower

    But if so, how do we find $\tq{\tth}{\tth}$ in the adjacent tower? We cannot “Gram–Schmidt” against a vector that is $0$!

    Resolution

    Nothing stops us from applying $J_-$ to $\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\tth}$ and working out the result symbolically. Only when we expand the symbolic result in the original basis do we find that it is $0$.

    Doing this: $$\begin{aligned}\text{LHS} &= J_-\tq{\fh}{\fh} = C^-(\fh,\fh)\,\tq{\fh}{\tth} = \sqrt5\,\tq{\fh}{\tth},\\[4pt] \text{RHS} &= J_-\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\tth} = \big[J_-,\Sl{+1}\big]\ket{\tth,\tth} + \Sl{+1}J_-\ket{\tth,\tth}\\ &= C^-(1,1)\,\Sl{0}\ket{\tth,\tth} + \Sl{+1}\,C^-(\tth,\tth)\ket{\tth,\h}\\ &= \sqrt2\,\Sl{0}\ket{\tth,\tth} + \sqrt3\,\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\h}.\end{aligned}$$ So $$\tq{\fh}{\tth} = \sqrt{\tfrac25}\,\Sl{0}\ket{\tth,\tth} + \sqrt{\tfrac35}\,\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\h} \;\;(=0).$$ $\tq{\tth}{\tth}$, being symbolically orthogonal to $\tq{\fh}{\tth}$, must then be (with the Condon–Shortley sign) $$\tq{\tth}{\tth} = \sqrt{\tfrac25}\,\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\h} - \sqrt{\tfrac35}\,\Sl{0}\ket{\tth,\tth},$$ which, although only “symbolically orthonormal”, is not $0$. Explicitly, $\Sl{+1}\ket{\tth,\h} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\ket{\U\U\U}$ and $\Sl{0}\ket{\tth,\tth}=\h\ket{\U\U\U}$, so $\tq{\tth}{\tth} = -\tfrac{\sqrt{15}}{6}\ket{\tth,\tth}$.

    Highest-weight construction for the $(\tfrac12,\alpha{=}2)$ irrep

    We now apply the highest-weight construction to the $(\h,\alpha{=}2)$ irrep. The steps are:

    1. Find the states $\ka{\pm\h}{2}$ (and the rest of the original basis) in the $\ket{\U\D\U}$ basis.
    2. Express the $\tk{\h}{m}$ and $\tk{\tth}{m}$ states in terms of the $\Sl{l}\ka{\pm\h}{2}$ states.
    3. Plug the results of step 1 into the results of step 2.
    4. Project onto the original irrep states to find the reduced matrix elements.

    The reason for first converting to the $\ket{\U\D\U}$ basis is that the operators $$\Sl{\pm1,0} = \Big\{\mp\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}S^{(2)}_\pm,\; S^{(2)}_3\Big\}$$ are easiest to evaluate in this basis.

    Step 1: the original basis in terms of single spins

    Write $\ket{j_{12},m_{12}}$ for the states of particles 1 and 2, and $\ket{\h,m}$ for particle 3. Start from $$\ket{\tth,\tth} = \underbrace{\ket{1,1}}_{(1)\text{ and }(2)}\underbrace{\ket{\h,\h}}_{(3)}$$ and lower both sides.

    LHS: $J_-\ket{\tth,\tth} = C^-(\tth,\tth)\ket{\tth,\h} = \sqrt3\,\ket{\tth,\h}$.

    RHS: $$\begin{aligned}\big(J_-\ket{1,1}\big)\ket{\h,\h} + \ket{1,1}\big(J_-\ket{\h,\h}\big) &= C^-(1,1)\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h} + C^-(\h,\h)\ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh}\\ &= \sqrt2\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h} + \ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh}.\end{aligned}$$ So $$\ket{\tth,\h} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh}.$$

    The states $\ka{\h}{1}$, $\ka{\h}{2}$ and $\ket{\tth,\h}$ live in the subspace spanned by $$\big\{\ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh},\; \ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h},\; \ket{0,0}\ket{\h,\h}\big\}.$$ So a convenient choice is $$\begin{aligned}\ka{\h}{1} &= \ket{0,0}\ket{\h,\h},\\ \ka{\h}{2} &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h}.\end{aligned}$$

    The remaining $\ket{\tth,m}$ states. We need $$C^-(\tth,\h)=\sqrt{\big(\tth+\h\big)\big(\tth-\h+1\big)}=\sqrt{2\times2}=2,\qquad C^-(1,0)=\sqrt{(1+0)(1-0+1)}=\sqrt2 .$$ Then $$\begin{aligned}\underbrace{J_-\ket{\tth,\h}}_{=\,2\ket{\tth,\mh}} &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\big(J_-\ket{1,0}\big)\ket{\h,\h} + \ket{1,0}\big(J_-\ket{\h,\h}\big)\Big) + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(\big(J_-\ket{1,1}\big)\ket{\h,\mh} + \ket{1,1}\underbrace{\big(J_-\ket{\h,\mh}\big)}_{=0}\Big)\\ &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(C^-(1,0)\ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\h} + C^-(\h,\h)\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh}\Big) + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,C^-(1,1)\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh}\\ &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\sqrt2\,\ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\h} + \ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh} + \ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh}\Big)\\ &= 2\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\h} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh}\Big).\end{aligned}$$ So $$\ket{\tth,\mh} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\h},$$ and of course $\ket{\tth,\mtth} = \ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\mh}$.

    The $m=-\tfrac12$ states of the two spin-$\tfrac12$ irreps. For $\alpha=1$, obviously $\ka{\mh}{1} = \ket{0,0}\ket{\h,\mh}$. For $\alpha=2$, lower $$\ka{\h}{2} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h}.$$ LHS: $J_-\ka{\h}{2} = C^-(\h,\h)\,\ka{\mh}{2} = \ka{\mh}{2}$.

    RHS: $$\begin{aligned}&\sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\big(J_-\ket{1,1}\big)\ket{\h,\mh} + \ket{1,1}\underbrace{\big(J_-\ket{\h,\mh}\big)}_{=0}\Big) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(\big(J_-\ket{1,0}\big)\ket{\h,\h} + \ket{1,0}\big(J_-\ket{\h,\h}\big)\Big)\\ &\quad= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\sqrt2\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh}\Big) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(\sqrt2\,\ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\h} + \ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh}\Big)\\ &\quad= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh} - \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\h}.\end{aligned}$$

    Tabulating in the $\ket{\U\D\U}$ basis. Using $\ket{1,1}=\ket{\U\U}$, $\ket{1,0}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D}+\ket{\D\U}\big)$, $\ket{1,-1}=\ket{\D\D}$, $\ket{0,0}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D}-\ket{\D\U}\big)$ for particles 1 and 2:

    $(\h,\alpha{=}1)$ irrep: $$\begin{aligned}\ka{\h}{1} &= \ket{0,0}\ket{\h,\h} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D}-\ket{\D\U}\big)\otimes\ket{\U} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D\U}-\ket{\D\U\U}\big),\\ \ka{\mh}{1} &= \ket{0,0}\ket{\h,\mh} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D\D}-\ket{\D\U\D}\big).\end{aligned}$$ $(\h,\alpha{=}2)$ irrep: $$\begin{aligned}\ka{\h}{2} &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{\U\U}\otimes\ket{\D} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D}+\ket{\D\U}\big)\otimes\ket{\U} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(-\ket{\U\D\U}-\ket{\D\U\U}+2\ket{\U\U\D}\big),\\ \ka{\mh}{2} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D}+\ket{\D\U}\big)\otimes\ket{\D} - \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{\D\D}\otimes\ket{\U} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\ket{\U\D\D}+\ket{\D\U\D}-2\ket{\D\D\U}\big).\end{aligned}$$ $(\tth)$ irrep: $$\begin{aligned}\ket{\tth,\tth} &= \ket{\U\U\U},\\ \ket{\tth,\h} &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D}+\ket{\D\U}\big)\otimes\ket{\U} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ket{\U\U}\otimes\ket{\D} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\big(\ket{\U\D\U}+\ket{\D\U\U}+\ket{\U\U\D}\big),\\ \ket{\tth,\mh} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\big(\ket{\U\D\D}+\ket{\D\U\D}+\ket{\D\D\U}\big),\\ \ket{\tth,\mtth} &= \ket{\D\D\D}.\end{aligned}$$

    Step 2: the tilde states in terms of $S^1_l\lvert\frac12,m,\alpha{=}2\rangle$

    The $\widetilde{J}=\tfrac32$ tower. The top state is $$\tk{\tth}{\tth} = \Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2}.$$ Lowering (as in the general construction): the LHS gives $J_-\tk{\tth}{\tth}=\sqrt3\,\tk{\tth}{\h}$ and the RHS gives $\sqrt2\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}$, so $$\tk{\tth}{\h} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}.$$ Lower again. LHS: $J_-\tk{\tth}{\h} = C^-(\tth,\h)\,\tk{\tth}{\mh} = 2\,\tk{\tth}{\mh}$, using $C^-(\tth,\h)=\sqrt{2\times2}=2$. RHS: $$\begin{aligned}&\sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\big[J_-,\Sl{0}\big]\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{0}J_-\ka{\h}{2}\Big)\\ &\qquad + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(\big[J_-,\Sl{+1}\big]\ka{\mh}{2} + \Sl{+1}J_-\ka{\mh}{2}\Big)\\ &\quad= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(C^-(1,0)\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} + C^-(\h,\h)\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}\Big)\\ &\qquad + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(C^-(1,1)\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2} + 0\Big)\\ &\quad= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\sqrt2\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}\Big)\\ &\qquad + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}.\end{aligned}$$ So $$\tk{\tth}{\mh} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2},$$ and of course $\tk{\tth}{\mtth} = \Sl{-1}\ka{\mh}{2}$.

    The $\widetilde{J}=\tfrac12$ tower. From the general construction, $$\tk{\h}{\h} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}.$$ Lower both sides. LHS: $J_-\tk{\h}{\h} = C^-(\h,\h)\,\tk{\h}{\mh} = \tk{\h}{\mh}$. RHS: $$\begin{aligned}&-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(\big[J_-,\Sl{0}\big]\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{0}J_-\ka{\h}{2}\Big)\\ &\qquad + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\big[J_-,\Sl{+1}\big]\ka{\mh}{2} + \Sl{+1}\underbrace{J_-\ka{\mh}{2}}_{=0}\Big)\\ &\quad= -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(\sqrt2\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} + \Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}\Big)\\ &\qquad + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(\sqrt2\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}\Big)\\ &\quad= -\sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}.\end{aligned}$$

    Summary of Step 2

    $$\begin{aligned}\tk{\tth}{\tth} &= \Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2}\\ \tk{\tth}{\h} &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}\\ \tk{\tth}{\mh} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}\\ \tk{\tth}{\mtth} &= \Sl{-1}\ka{\mh}{2}\\[4pt] \tk{\h}{\h} &= -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}\\ \tk{\h}{\mh} &= -\sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2}\end{aligned}$$

    Step 3: combining Steps 1 and 2

    Recall from the conventions that $\Sl{\pm1} = \mp\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}S^{(2)}_\pm$, so evaluating $\Sl{\pm1}$ on single spins brings in an additional signed factor of $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$ relative to $C^\pm(j,m)$.

    The $\widetilde{J}=\tfrac32$ tower. Let us calculate $\tk{\tth}{\tth}$ and $\tk{\tth}{\h}$ in the $\ket{\U\D\U}$ basis first; we will notice a pattern. $$\begin{aligned}\tk{\tth}{\tth} &= \Sl{+1}\ka{\h}{2} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}S^{(2)}_+\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(-\ket{\U\D\U}\underbrace{-\ket{\D\U\U}+2\ket{\U\U\D}}_{\text{annihilated by }S^{(2)}_+}\big)\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,S^{(2)}_+\ket{\U\D\U} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,\ket{\U\U\U} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,\ket{\tth,\tth}.\end{aligned}$$ $$\begin{aligned}\tk{\tth}{\h} &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}\\ &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,S^{(2)}_3\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(-\ket{\U\D\U}-\ket{\D\U\U}+2\ket{\U\U\D}\big)\Big)\\ &\qquad + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}S^{(2)}_+\Big)\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\ket{\U\D\D}+\ket{\D\U\D}-2\ket{\D\D\U}\big)\Big)\\ &= \sqrt{\tfrac23}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\Big(\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\U}-\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\U}+\ket{\U\U\D}\Big) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\Big(\ket{\U\U\D}-2\ket{\D\U\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac13\Big(\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\U}-\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\U}+\ket{\U\U\D}\Big) - \tfrac16\Big(\ket{\U\U\D}-2\ket{\D\U\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac16\Big(\ket{\U\D\U}-\ket{\D\U\U}+2\ket{\U\U\D}-\ket{\U\U\D}+2\ket{\D\U\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac16\Big(\ket{\U\D\U}+\ket{\D\U\U}+\ket{\U\U\D}\Big) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\Big(\ket{\U\D\U}+\ket{\D\U\U}+\ket{\U\U\D}\Big) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,\ket{\tth,\h}.\end{aligned}$$

    Two observations
    1. $\tk{\tth}{M}$ is proportional to $\ket{\tth,M}$ only. It must be: there is no other state in the original $\h\oplus\h\oplus\tth$ irreps that transforms like $\tk{\tth}{M}$.
    2. The proportionality constant is $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}$ regardless of $M$. Reason: if $\tk{\tth}{\tth} = k\,\ket{\tth,\tth}$, then applying $J_-$ to both sides pulls out the same $C^-(j,m)$ (here $C^-(\tth,\tth)$) on each side, so we get $\tk{\tth}{\h} = k\,\ket{\tth,\h}$ with the same $k$, and so on down the ladder. The same story holds if the RHS has several $k$'s.

    So we conclude $$\tk{\tth}{M} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,\ket{\tth,M}.$$

    The $\widetilde{J}=\tfrac12$ tower. Now let us move on to the $\tk{\h}{\pm\h}$ states. $$\begin{aligned}\tk{\h}{\h} &= -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\h}{2} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{+1}\ka{\mh}{2}\\ &= -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,S^{(2)}_3\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(-\ket{\U\D\U}-\ket{\D\U\U}+2\ket{\U\U\D}\big)\Big)\\ &\qquad + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\Big(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}S^{(2)}_+\Big)\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\ket{\U\D\D}+\ket{\D\U\D}-2\ket{\D\D\U}\big)\Big)\\ &= -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\U}-\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\U}+\ket{\U\U\D}\Big) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(\ket{\U\U\D}-2\ket{\D\U\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\U} - 2\ket{\U\U\D} + \tfrac52\ket{\D\U\U}\Big).\end{aligned}$$ $\tk{\h}{\h}$ must be a linear combination of the $\ka{\h}{1}$ and $\ka{\h}{2}$ states. Find the coefficients by calculating inner products: $$\begin{aligned}\big\langle \h,\h,\alpha{=}1 \big\vert \widetilde{\h,\h}\big\rangle^{(2)} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\bra{\U\D\U}-\bra{\D\U\U}\big)\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\U}-2\ket{\U\U\D}+\tfrac52\ket{\D\U\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\cdot\big(-\tfrac12\big) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\cdot\tfrac52 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{36}}\,(-3) = -\tfrac12,\\[6pt] \big\langle \h,\h,\alpha{=}2 \big\vert \widetilde{\h,\h}\big\rangle^{(2)} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(-\bra{\U\D\U}-\bra{\D\U\U}+2\bra{\U\U\D}\big)\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\U}-2\ket{\U\U\D}+\tfrac52\ket{\D\U\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(\tfrac12-4-\tfrac52\Big) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{108}}\,(-6) = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}.\end{aligned}$$ So $$\tk{\h}{\h} = -\tfrac12\,\ka{\h}{1} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ka{\h}{2}.$$

    As discussed in point (b) above, if $\tk{\h}{\h} = -\tfrac12\,\ka{\h}{1} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ka{\h}{2}$, then we expect $$\tk{\h}{\mh} = -\tfrac12\,\ka{\mh}{1} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\ka{\mh}{2}.$$ Let us check this explicitly: $$\begin{aligned}\tk{\h}{\mh} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2} - \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\Sl{-1}\ka{\h}{2}\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,S^{(2)}_3\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\ket{\U\D\D}+\ket{\D\U\D}-2\ket{\D\D\U}\big)\Big)\\ &\qquad - \sqrt{\tfrac23}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}S^{(2)}_-\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(-\ket{\U\D\U}-\ket{\D\U\U}+2\ket{\U\U\D}\big)\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\D}+\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\D}+\ket{\D\D\U}\Big) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(0-\ket{\D\D\U}+2\ket{\U\D\D}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac52\ket{\U\D\D}+\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\D}+2\ket{\D\D\U}\Big).\end{aligned}$$ So $$\begin{aligned}\big\langle \h,\mh,\alpha{=}1 \big\vert \widetilde{\h,\mh}\big\rangle^{(2)} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\bra{\U\D\D}-\bra{\D\U\D}\big)\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac52\ket{\U\D\D}+\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\D}+2\ket{\D\D\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac52-\tfrac12\Big) = -\tfrac{3}{\sqrt{36}} = -\tfrac12,\\[6pt] \big\langle \h,\mh,\alpha{=}2 \big\vert \widetilde{\h,\mh}\big\rangle^{(2)} &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\bra{\U\D\D}+\bra{\D\U\D}-2\bra{\D\D\U}\big)\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac52\ket{\U\D\D}+\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\D}+2\ket{\D\D\U}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{18}}\Big(-\tfrac52+\tfrac12-4\Big) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\cdot\tfrac16\cdot(-6) = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}.\end{aligned}$$ This agrees with the claim above. ✓

    Note that the tilde states are not unit vectors: $$\big\lVert\tk{\tth}{M}\big\rVert^2 = \tfrac1{12},\qquad \big\lVert\tk{\h}{M}\big\rVert^2 = \big(\tfrac12\big)^2+\big(\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\big)^2 = \tfrac{7}{12}.$$ The symbolic orthonormality of Route 2 is not inherited by the actual vectors; only their mutual orthogonality is.

    Reduced matrix elements. Therefore: $$\tk{\tth}{M} = k^{3/2,\,1/2}_{2,\,3/2}\,\ket{\tth,M} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,\ket{\tth,M},$$ $$\begin{aligned}\tk{\h}{M} &= k^{1/2,\,1/2}_{2,\,\beta=1}\,\kb{M}{1} + k^{1/2,\,1/2}_{2,\,\beta=2}\,\kb{M}{2}\\ &= -\tfrac12\,\kb{M}{1} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\kb{M}{2}.\end{aligned}$$

    The reduced matrix element $k^{J,j}_{\alpha,\beta}$

    In $k^{J,j}_{\alpha,\beta}$:

    • $\alpha$ is the degeneracy index of the irrep used in the highest-weight construction (HWC). Here $\alpha=2$: the tilde states come from the HWC on the $\alpha=2$ spin-$\h$ irrep.
    • $\beta$ is the degeneracy index of the irreps in the original basis that the tilde states land in. The $\tth$ irrep occurs only once, so it is labelled simply by its spin, $\beta=\tth$.
    • $J$ is the total spin of the tilde states, i.e. of (tensor operator) $\otimes$ (HWC irrep), which is also the spin of the original basis states they land in.
    • $j$ is the total spin of the HWC irrep.

    Step 4: connecting back to matrix elements

    Ultimately we want to compute matrix elements $$\big\langle J,M,\beta \big\vert\, \Sl{l} \,\big\vert j,m,\alpha \big\rangle ,$$ and we want to connect these quantities to the reduced matrix elements $k^{J,j}_{\alpha,\beta}$.

    Recall the “isomorphism” $$O^s_l\ket{j,m} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \ket{s,l}\ket{j,m}, \qquad\text{which implies}\qquad \lvert\widetilde{J,M}\rangle \;\longleftrightarrow\; \ket{J,M}.$$ Therefore the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients relate both $\ket{J,M}$ to $\ket{s,l}\ket{j,m}$ and $\lvert\widetilde{J,M}\rangle$ to $O^s_l\ket{j,m}$; only linearity of the correspondence is needed for this. So using Clebsch–Gordan coefficients we can express $\lvert\widetilde{J,M}\rangle$ in terms of $O^s_l\ket{j,m}$, and vice versa.

    So the plan is:

    1. Express $O^s_l\ket{j,m}$ in terms of the $\lvert\widetilde{J,M}\rangle$ states.
    2. Expand the $\lvert\widetilde{J,M}\rangle$ states in terms of the $k^{J,j}_{\alpha,\beta}$ and the original $\ket{J,M,\beta}$ states.
    3. Hit both sides with $\bra{J,M,\beta}$.

    Step 1 of the plan. Copy the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients for $1\otimes\h$ (rows $\ket{J,M}$, columns $\ket{1,l}\ket{\h,m}$): $$\begin{array}{c|cccccc} & \ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\h} & \ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh} & \ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h} & \ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh} & \ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\h} & \ket{1,-1}\ket{\h,\mh}\\ \hline \ket{\tth,\tth} & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\\ \ket{\tth,\h} & 0 & \sqrt{\tfrac13} & \sqrt{\tfrac23} & 0 & 0 & 0\\ \ket{\tth,\mh} & 0 & 0 & 0 & \sqrt{\tfrac23} & \sqrt{\tfrac13} & 0\\ \ket{\tth,\mtth} & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\\ \ket{\h,\h} & 0 & \sqrt{\tfrac23} & -\sqrt{\tfrac13} & 0 & 0 & 0\\ \ket{\h,\mh} & 0 & 0 & 0 & \sqrt{\tfrac13} & -\sqrt{\tfrac23} & 0 \end{array}$$ Reading along a row gives a coupled state in terms of product states; for example $$\ket{\tth,\h} = \sqrt{\tfrac13}\,\ket{1,1}\ket{\h,\mh} + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\h}.$$ The matrix is real orthogonal, so reading down a column gives the inverse expansion; for example $$\ket{1,0}\ket{\h,\mh} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\ket{\tth,\mh} + \sqrt{\tfrac13}\,\ket{\h,\mh}.$$ And by the “isomorphism” we have, for example, $$\Sl{0}\ka{\mh}{2} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,\tk{\tth}{\mh} + \sqrt{\tfrac13}\,\tk{\h}{\mh}.$$

    Steps 2 and 3 of the plan. Now plug in the expansion in terms of reduced matrix elements: $$\begin{aligned}\underbrace{\Sl{0}}_{l}\,\big\vert\underbrace{\tfrac12}_{j},\underbrace{-\tfrac12}_{m},\alpha{=}2\big\rangle &= \underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac23}}_{\text{CG}}\Big(\underbrace{k^{J=3/2,\,j=1/2}_{\alpha=2,\,\beta=3/2}}_{\text{RME}}\,\big\vert\underbrace{\tfrac32}_{J},-\tfrac12\big\rangle\Big)\\ &\quad+ \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}}_{\text{CG}}\Big(\underbrace{k^{J=1/2,\,j=1/2}_{\alpha=2,\,\beta=1}}_{\text{RME}}\,\kb{\mh}{1} + \underbrace{k^{J=1/2,\,j=1/2}_{\alpha=2,\,\beta=2}}_{\text{RME}}\,\kb{\mh}{2}\Big).\end{aligned}$$ So, for example, if we hit this with $\big\langle J{=}\tfrac12,M{=}{-}\tfrac12,\beta{=}1\big\vert$, we get $$\begin{aligned}&\big\langle J{=}\tfrac12,M{=}{-}\tfrac12,\beta{=}1 \big\vert\, \Sl{0} \,\big\vert j{=}\tfrac12,m{=}{-}\tfrac12,\alpha{=}2\big\rangle\\[2pt] &\qquad= \underbrace{\big\langle J{=}\tfrac12,M{=}{-}\tfrac12 \,\big\vert\, s{=}1,l{=}0;\, j{=}\tfrac12,m{=}{-}\tfrac12\big\rangle}_{=\,1/\sqrt3}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{k^{J=1/2,\,j=1/2}_{\alpha=2,\,\beta=1}}_{=\,-1/2}\\[4pt] &\qquad= \big\langle J{=}\tfrac12,M{=}{-}\tfrac12 \,\big\vert\, s{=}1,l{=}0;\, j{=}\tfrac12,m{=}{-}\tfrac12\big\rangle\, \big\langle J{=}\tfrac12,\beta{=}1 \,\big\Vert\, S^1 \,\big\Vert\, j{=}\tfrac12,\alpha{=}2\big\rangle,\end{aligned}$$ exactly as in the statement of the Wigner–Eckart theorem. As a direct check, $$S^{(2)}_3\ka{\mh}{2} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\Big(-\tfrac12\ket{\U\D\D}+\tfrac12\ket{\D\U\D}+\ket{\D\D\U}\Big),$$ whose overlap with $\ka{\mh}{1}=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\U\D\D}-\ket{\D\U\D}\big)$ is $$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\Big(-\tfrac12-\tfrac12\Big) = -\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt3} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\cdot\Big(-\tfrac12\Big). \;\checkmark$$

    Conclusion

    $k^{J,j}_{\alpha,\beta}$ as defined above is exactly the reduced matrix element $$k^{J,j}_{\alpha,\beta} = \big\langle J,\beta \,\big\Vert\, O^s \,\big\Vert\, j,\alpha \big\rangle$$ as it appears in the Wigner–Eckart theorem.
