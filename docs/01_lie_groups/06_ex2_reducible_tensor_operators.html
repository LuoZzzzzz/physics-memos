Wigner–Eckart Examples: Reducible Tensor Operators

3 October 2026
Digitised from handwritten notes.

Contents

    Setting and conventions

    Conventions
    • A single spinless particle, with $[r_a,p_b]=i\delta_{ab}$. The rotation generators are the orbital angular momentum, $J_a=\varepsilon_{acd}\,r_cp_d$.
    • A set of operators $O_x$ is a tensor operator in representation $D$ if $[J_a,O_x]=O_y\,\big[J^D_a\big]_{yx}$. The adjoint (spin-1, Cartesian) representation is $\big[J^{\text{adj}}_a\big]_{cb}=-i\varepsilon_{acb}$.
    • Spherical components of a vector operator $\vec v$: $\tilde v^1_0=v_3$, $\tilde v^1_{\pm1}=\mp\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(v_1\pm iv_2)$.
    • Ladder operators $J_\pm=J_1\pm iJ_2$, with $C^\pm(j,m)=\sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}$. For a spin-$s$ tensor operator in standard form, $\big[J_\pm,\tilde O^s_l\big]=C^\pm(s,l)\,\tilde O^s_{l\pm1}$.
    • “$\ket{J,M}\simeq X$” means that the operator $X$ transforms under commutation with $J_a$ exactly as the state $\ket{J,M}$ transforms under $J_a$.

    $\vec r$ and $\vec p$ are tensor operators

    Both $\vec r$ and $\vec p$ are tensor operators: $$\begin{aligned}{}[J_a,r_b] &= \varepsilon_{acd}\,[r_cp_d,\,r_b] = -i\varepsilon_{adb}\,r_d = i\varepsilon_{abd}\,r_d,\\ [J_a,p_b] &= \varepsilon_{acd}\,[r_cp_d,\,p_b] = -i\varepsilon_{adb}\,p_d = i\varepsilon_{abd}\,p_d.\end{aligned}$$ Recall also the Leibniz rule: $$[A,BC] = ABC-BCA = ABC-BAC+BAC-BCA = [A,B]\,C + B\,[A,C].$$

    The nine operators $T_{bc}=r_bp_c$

    Now consider the operators $$T_{bc} = r_bp_c,\qquad b,c\in\{1,2,3\}.$$ Their commutator with $J_a$ is, by the Leibniz rule with $A=J_a$, $B=r_b$, $C=p_c$, $$\begin{aligned}{}[J_a,T_{bc}] = [J_a,\,r_bp_c] &= [J_a,r_b]\,p_c + r_b\,[J_a,p_c]\\ &= i\varepsilon_{abd}\,r_dp_c + r_b\,\big(i\varepsilon_{acd}\,p_d\big)\\ &= i\varepsilon_{abd}\,T_{dc} + i\varepsilon_{acd}\,T_{bd}.\end{aligned}$$

    Comparison with a product state. If $J^D_a = J^{D_1}_a + J^{D_2}_a$, i.e. $J^D_a = J^{D_1}_a\otimes 1 + 1\otimes J^{D_2}_a$, and $\ket{b}\in D_1$, $\ket{c}\in D_2$, then $$\begin{aligned}J^D_a\ket{b}\ket{c} &= \big[J^{D_1}_a\big]_{b'b}\,\ket{b'}\ket{c} + \big[J^{D_2}_a\big]_{c'c}\,\ket{b}\ket{c'}\\ &= \underbrace{\big[J^{D_1}_a\big]_{db}}_{\substack{i\varepsilon_{abd}\\ =\,-i\varepsilon_{adb}}}\underbrace{\ket{d}\ket{c}}_{T_{dc}} \;+\; \underbrace{\big[J^{D_2}_a\big]_{dc}}_{\substack{i\varepsilon_{acd}\\ =\,-i\varepsilon_{adc}}}\underbrace{\ket{b}\ket{d}}_{T_{bd}} ,\end{aligned}$$ where the underbraces show what each factor corresponds to when $D_1=D_2=\text{adj}$.

    $T_{bc}$ transforms like a two-particle state

    $T_{bc}=r_bp_c$ transforms exactly as a state $\ket{b}\ket{c}$ would in the product of two adjoint representations, $\text{adj}\otimes\text{adj}$, whose generators are $$J_a = J^{(1)}_a + J^{(2)}_a = J^{\text{adj}}_a\otimes 1 + 1\otimes J^{\text{adj}}_a .$$ That is, $[J_a,T_{bc}] = T_{de}\,\big[J^{\text{adj}\otimes\text{adj}}_a\big]_{de,\,bc}$, a 9-dimensional and reducible representation.

    The expected structure

    Since $\vec r$ and $\vec p$ are individually spin-1 tensor operators, we expect to find linear combinations of the $r_bp_c$ that obey the structure $$1\otimes1 = 0\oplus1\oplus2$$ under commutation with $J_a$. So, for example, the spin-0 piece should satisfy $$[J_a,T_{0,0}] = 0,\qquad\text{as}\quad \big[J^{s=0}_a\big]_{bc}=0\ \text{for all valid } b,c$$ (and there is just one valid pair, since the spin-0 representation is 1-dimensional).

    The nine operators are to be reorganised as follows: $$\underbrace{\begin{array}{ccc} T_{11} & T_{12} & T_{13}\\ T_{21} & T_{22} & T_{23}\\ T_{31} & T_{32} & T_{33} \end{array}}_{\text{original (adj}\otimes\text{adj) basis}} \qquad\longrightarrow\qquad \underbrace{ \boxed{\begin{array}{c} T_{0,0}\end{array}} \quad \boxed{\begin{array}{c} T_{1,-1}\\ T_{1,0}\\ T_{1,+1}\end{array}} \quad \boxed{\begin{array}{c} T_{2,-2}\\ T_{2,-1}\\ T_{2,0}\\ T_{2,+1}\\ T_{2,+2}\end{array}} }_{0\,\oplus\,1\,\oplus\,2\ \text{basis}}$$ Each box is self-contained under $[J_a,\,\cdot\,]$: the first transforms like spin 0, the second like spin 1, the third like spin 2.

    The spin-0 piece

    Consider the trace $$T_{bb} = T_{11}+T_{22}+T_{33} = \rdp .$$ Its commutator is $$[J_a,T_{bb}] = i\varepsilon_{abd}\,\big(T_{db}+T_{bd}\big).$$ Here $\varepsilon_{abd}$ is antisymmetric under $b\leftrightarrow d$ while $T_{db}+T_{bd}$ is symmetric under $b\leftrightarrow d$, so the sum vanishes after contracting: the $(b,d)=(1,2)$ term cancels the $(2,1)$ term, $(1,3)$ cancels $(3,1)$, and so on. Hence $$[J_a,\,\rdp] = \big[J^{s=0}_a\big]_{00}\,(\rdp) = 0,$$ and $\rdp$ is the spin-0 piece.

    The spin-1 piece

    Let $$A_{ab} = \tfrac12\big(T_{ab}-T_{ba}\big) = \tfrac12\big(r_ap_b-r_bp_a\big) = \tfrac12\,\varepsilon_{abc}\,J_c .$$ As we sweep $a,b$ over $\{1,2,3\}$ we find the independent operators $\tfrac12(J_1,J_2,J_3)$. And we already know that these operators, constructed from $\vec r$ and $\vec p$, satisfy $$[J_a,J_b] = i\varepsilon_{abc}\,J_c = -i\varepsilon_{acb}\,J_c = \big[J^{\text{adj}}_a\big]_{cb}\,J_c .$$ From before, we also know $[J_a,r_b] = \big[J^{\text{adj}}_a\big]_{cb}\,r_c$, so $J_b$ and $r_b$ share the same structure. We also know that to put $r_1,r_2,r_3$ in the spin-1 form we take $$\rt{0}=r_3,\qquad \rt{+1}=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(r_1+ir_2),\qquad \rt{-1}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(r_1-ir_2).$$ So to put $J_1,J_2,J_3$ in the appropriate spin-1 form we take $$\Jt{0}=J_3,\qquad \Jt{+1} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(J_1+iJ_2) = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,J_+,\qquad \Jt{-1} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(J_1-iJ_2) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,J_- .$$

    So far. Using $J_1 = r_2p_3-r_3p_2$, $J_2 = r_3p_1-r_1p_3$ and $J_3 = r_1p_2-r_2p_1$: $$\begin{aligned}T^0_0 &\propto \rdp = r_1p_1+r_2p_2+r_3p_3,\\ T^1_0 &\propto J_3 = r_1p_2-r_2p_1,\\ T^1_{+1} &\propto \Jt{+1} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(J_1+iJ_2) = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big((r_2p_3-r_3p_2)+i(r_3p_1-r_1p_3)\Big)\\ &\phantom{\propto \Jt{+1}} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,\big(r_2p_3-r_3p_2+ir_3p_1-ir_1p_3\big),\\ T^1_{-1} &\propto \Jt{-1} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(J_1-iJ_2) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,\big(r_2p_3-r_3p_2-ir_3p_1+ir_1p_3\big).\end{aligned}$$

    The spin-2 piece

    In the spherical basis we have $$\big[J_3,\rt{l_1}\big] = l_1\,\rt{l_1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \big[J_3,\pt{l_2}\big] = l_2\,\pt{l_2}.$$ Hence, under commutation with $J_3$, the operator $\rt{l_1}\pt{l_2}$ behaves as $$\begin{aligned}\big[J_3,\,\rt{l_1}\pt{l_2}\big] &= \big[J_3,\rt{l_1}\big]\,\pt{l_2} + \rt{l_1}\,\big[J_3,\pt{l_2}\big]\\ &= l_1\,\rt{l_1}\pt{l_2} + \rt{l_1}\,\big(l_2\,\pt{l_2}\big)\\ &= (l_1+l_2)\,\rt{l_1}\pt{l_2}.\end{aligned}$$ If $l_1=l_2=+1$ then $\big[J_3,\rt{+1}\pt{+1}\big] = 2\,\rt{+1}\pt{+1}$, and this is the only element of the tensor operator with a $J_3$ value of 2. So if there really is a spin-2 piece, this must be its top state.

    Let us suppose it is the $\ket{2,2}$ state. Then commuting it with $J_-$ should produce 4 new operators, $\ket{2,1},\dots,\ket{2,-2}$. Their coefficient vectors in the $\rt{l_1}\pt{l_2}$ basis are orthonormal to one another and to those of the spin-0 and spin-1 pieces. This is orthonormality in the symbolic sense, via the correspondence $\rt{l_1}\pt{l_2}\leftrightarrow\ket{1,l_1}\ket{1,l_2}$.

    For convenience we label operators using angular-momentum notation, so our hypothesis is $$\boxed{\;\ket{2,2}\overset{?}{\simeq}\rt{+1}\pt{+1}\;}$$

    Lower $\ket{2,2}$. Using $C^-(2,2)=\sqrt{4\times1}=2$ and $C^-(1,1)=\sqrt{2\times1}=\sqrt2$: $$\begin{aligned}\underbrace{J_-\ket{2,2}}_{C^-(2,2)\ket{2,1}\,=\,2\ket{2,1}} \;\simeq\; \big[J_-,\,\rt{+1}\pt{+1}\big] &= \big[J_-,\rt{+1}\big]\,\pt{+1} + \rt{+1}\,\big[J_-,\pt{+1}\big]\\ &= C^-(1,1)\,\rt{0}\pt{+1} + \rt{+1}\,\big(C^-(1,1)\,\pt{0}\big)\\ &= \sqrt2\,\big(\rt{0}\pt{+1} + \rt{+1}\pt{0}\big).\end{aligned}$$ So $$\boxed{\;\ket{2,1}\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,\big(\rt{0}\pt{+1}+\rt{+1}\pt{0}\big)\;}$$

    Lower $\ket{2,1}$. Using $C^-(2,1)=\sqrt{(2+1)(2-1+1)}=\sqrt6$: $$\begin{aligned}\underbrace{J_-\ket{2,1}}_{\sqrt6\,\ket{2,0}} \;\simeq\; \Big[J_-,\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\rt{0}\pt{+1}+\rt{+1}\pt{0}\big)\Big] &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(\big[J_-,\rt{0}\pt{+1}\big] + \big[J_-,\rt{+1}\pt{0}\big]\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(\underbrace{\big[J_-,\rt{0}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\rt{-1}}\pt{+1} + \rt{0}\underbrace{\big[J_-,\pt{+1}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\pt{0}}\\ &\hspace{3.2em} + \underbrace{\big[J_-,\rt{+1}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\rt{0}}\pt{0} + \rt{+1}\underbrace{\big[J_-,\pt{0}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\pt{-1}}\Big)\\ &= \rt{-1}\pt{+1} + 2\,\rt{0}\pt{0} + \rt{+1}\pt{-1}.\end{aligned}$$ So $$\boxed{\;\ket{2,0}\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\,\big(\rt{-1}\pt{+1}+\rt{+1}\pt{-1}+2\,\rt{0}\pt{0}\big)\;}$$

    Lower $\ket{2,0}$ again. Using $C^-(2,0)=\sqrt{(2+0)(2-0+1)}=\sqrt6$: $$\begin{aligned}\underbrace{J_-\ket{2,0}}_{\sqrt6\,\ket{2,-1}} &\simeq \Big[J_-,\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\rt{+1}\pt{-1}+\rt{-1}\pt{+1}+2\,\rt{0}\pt{0}\big)\Big]\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\Big(\big[J_-,\rt{+1}\pt{-1}\big] + \big[J_-,\rt{-1}\pt{+1}\big] + 2\,\big[J_-,\rt{0}\pt{0}\big]\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\Big(\underbrace{\big[J_-,\rt{+1}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\rt{0}}\pt{-1} + \rt{+1}\underbrace{\big[J_-,\pt{-1}\big]}_{0} + \underbrace{\big[J_-,\rt{-1}\big]}_{0}\pt{+1} + \rt{-1}\underbrace{\big[J_-,\pt{+1}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\pt{0}}\\ &\hspace{3.2em} + 2\,\underbrace{\big[J_-,\rt{0}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\rt{-1}}\pt{0} + 2\,\rt{0}\underbrace{\big[J_-,\pt{0}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\pt{-1}}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\Big(\sqrt2\,\rt{0}\pt{-1} + \sqrt2\,\rt{-1}\pt{0} + 2\sqrt2\,\rt{-1}\pt{0} + 2\sqrt2\,\rt{0}\pt{-1}\Big)\\ &= \sqrt3\,\big(\rt{0}\pt{-1} + \rt{-1}\pt{0}\big).\end{aligned}$$ So $$\boxed{\;\ket{2,-1}\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,\big(\rt{0}\pt{-1}+\rt{-1}\pt{0}\big)\;}$$

    Finally, lower $\ket{2,-1}$. Using $C^-(2,-1)=\sqrt{(2-1)(2+1+1)}=2$: $$\begin{aligned}\underbrace{J_-\ket{2,-1}}_{2\,\ket{2,-2}} \;\simeq\; \Big[J_-,\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\rt{0}\pt{-1}+\rt{-1}\pt{0}\big)\Big] &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(\underbrace{\big[J_-,\rt{0}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\rt{-1}}\pt{-1} + \rt{0}\underbrace{\big[J_-,\pt{-1}\big]}_{0}\\ &\hspace{3.2em} + \underbrace{\big[J_-,\rt{-1}\big]}_{0}\pt{0} + \rt{-1}\underbrace{\big[J_-,\pt{0}\big]}_{\sqrt2\,\pt{-1}}\Big)\\ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(2\sqrt2\,\rt{-1}\pt{-1}\Big) = 2\,\rt{-1}\pt{-1}.\end{aligned}$$ So $$\boxed{\;\ket{2,-2}\simeq\rt{-1}\pt{-1}\;}$$

    The hypothesis holds

    $\rt{+1}\pt{+1}$ is a genuine top state: $\big[J_+,\rt{+1}\big]=\big[J_+,\pt{+1}\big]=0$, so $\big[J_+,\rt{+1}\pt{+1}\big]=0$ by the Leibniz rule. Likewise $\big[J_-,\rt{-1}\pt{-1}\big]=0$, so the ladder closes after five states. Together with the one spin-0 and three spin-1 operators, this accounts for all $1+3+5=9$ of the $r_ap_b$.

    Summary

    The operators $r_ap_b$ may be reorganised as $$\begin{array}{ccccc} & & & & \ket{2,2}\simeq\rt{+1}\pt{+1}\\ & & \ket{1,1}\simeq\Jt{+1}=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}J_+ & & \ket{2,1}\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\rt{+1}\pt{0}+\rt{0}\pt{+1}\big)\\ \ket{0,0}\simeq\rdp & \oplus & \ket{1,0}\simeq J_3 & \oplus & \ket{2,0}\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}\big(\rt{+1}\pt{-1}+\rt{-1}\pt{+1}+2\,\rt{0}\pt{0}\big)\\ & & \ket{1,-1}\simeq\Jt{-1}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}J_- & & \ket{2,-1}\simeq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\rt{0}\pt{-1}+\rt{-1}\pt{0}\big)\\ & & & & \ket{2,-2}\simeq\rt{-1}\pt{-1} \end{array}$$ where $\rt{0}=r_3$ and $\rt{\pm1}=\mp\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(r_1\pm ir_2)$, and likewise for $\vec p$.

    The overall normalisation of each block is free, since any multiple of a block transforms the same way. For comparison, the Clebsch–Gordan combinations $(\tilde r\otimes\tilde p)_{J,M}=\sum_{l}\langle J,M\vert 1,l;\,1,M-l\rangle\,\rt{l}\,\pt{M-l}$ give exactly the spin-2 entries above, together with $$(\tilde r\otimes\tilde p)_{0,0} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\,\rdp,\qquad (\tilde r\otimes\tilde p)_{1,M} = \tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}\,\Jt{M}.$$
