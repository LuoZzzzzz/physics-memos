Memo 4 showed that the position r\vec rr is a spin-1 tensor operator, and the same argument works for the momentum p.\vec p\text{.}p. What about their products rbpc?r_bp_c\text{?}rb​pc​? There are nine of them, and commuting them with JaJ_aJa​ only mixes them among themselves, so they form a nine-dimensional representation. Since r\vec rr and p\vec pp each carry spin 1, the nine products should carry 1⊗1=0⊕1⊕2:1\otimes1=0\oplus1\oplus2\text{:}1⊗1=0⊕1⊕2: a scalar, a vector, and a five-component spin-2 object.

This memo finds all three pieces explicitly. Two of them turn out to be old friends, and the third is built by exactly the lowering procedure that produces Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, but with commutators in place of states.

4The expected structure

Since r\vec rr and p\vec pp are each spin-1 tensor operators, we expect to find combinations of the rbpcr_bp_crb​pc​ that obey the structure

1⊗1=0⊕1⊕21\otimes1=0\oplus1\oplus21⊗1=0⊕1⊕2
(8)

under commutation with Ja.J_a\text{.}Ja​. In the spherical basis you can see the count directly (Fig. 1). The product r~l11p~l21\rt{l_1}\pt{l_2}r~l1​1​p~​l2​1​ has J3J_3J3​-weight M=l1+l2M=l_1+l_2M=l1​+l2​ (we’ll show this in Section 7), and the nine products have weights 2,1,1,0,0,0,−1,−1,−2.2,1,1,0,0,0,-1,-1,-2\text{.}2,1,1,0,0,0,−1,−1,−2. A spin-2 tower uses one state at each of the five weights, a spin-1 tower one at each of the middle three, and a spin-0 piece the one that’s left at weight 0.Figure 1 For example, the spin-0 piece should satisfy

[Ja,T0,0]=0,[J_a,T_{0,0}] = 0,[Ja​,T0,0​]=0,
(9)

because the spin-0 representation is one-dimensional, with [Jas=0]00=0.\big[J^{s=0}_a\big]_{00}=0\text{.}[Jas=0​]00​=0. So the nine operators should reorganize like this:

Each box is self-contained under [Ja, ⋅ ]:[J_a,\,\cdot\,]\text{:}[Ja​,⋅]: the first transforms like spin 0, the second like spin 1, and the third like spin 2.

5The spin-0 piece

Consider the trace,

Tbb=T11+T22+T33=r⋅p.T_{bb} = T_{11}+T_{22}+T_{33} = \rdp .Tbb​=T11​+T22​+T33​=r⋅p.
(11)

Setting c=bc=bc=b in eq. (4) and summing, its commutator is

[Ja,Tbb]=iεabd (Tdb+Tbd).[J_a,T_{bb}] = i\varepsilon_{abd}\,\big(T_{db}+T_{bd}\big).[Ja​,Tbb​]=iεabd​(Tdb​+Tbd​).
(12)

Here εabd\varepsilon_{abd}εabd​ is antisymmetric under b↔d,b\leftrightarrow d\text{,}b↔d, while Tdb+TbdT_{db}+T_{bd}Tdb​+Tbd​ is symmetric, so the sum vanishes: the (b,d)=(1,2)(b,d)=(1,2)(b,d)=(1,2) term cancels the (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) term, (1,3)(1,3)(1,3) cancels (3,1),(3,1)\text{,}(3,1), and so on. [Ja, r⋅p]=0,[J_a,\,\rdp] = 0,[Ja​,r⋅p]=0,
(13)

and r⋅p\rdpr⋅p is the spin-0 piece. That makes sense: r⋅p\rdpr⋅p is a dot product, so it shouldn’t care which way the axes point.

2. Does the order matter?

Since pbrc=rcpb−iδbc,p_br_c=r_cp_b-i\delta_{bc}\text{,}pb​rc​=rc​pb​−iδbc​, we have Ubc=Tcb−iδbc.U_{bc}=T_{cb}-i\delta_{bc}\text{.}Ubc​=Tcb​−iδbc​. Then:

  • the trace is Ubb=Tbb−3i,U_{bb}=T_{bb}-3i\text{,}Ubb​=Tbb​−3i, so p⋅r=r⋅p−3i:\vec p\cdot\vec r=\rdp-3i\text{:}p⋅r=r⋅p−3i: the spin-0 piece shifts by a constant (which still commutes with everything);
  • the antisymmetric part is 12(Ubc−Ucb)=12(Tcb−Tbc)=−Abc,\tfrac12(U_{bc}-U_{cb})=\tfrac12(T_{cb}-T_{bc})=-A_{bc}\text{,}21​(Ubc​−Ucb​)=21​(Tcb​−Tbc​)=−Abc​, so the spin-1 piece is −J-\vec J−J instead of J;\vec J\text{;}J; indeed p×r=−J;\vec p\times\vec r=-\vec J\text{;}p×r=−J;
  • the symmetric traceless part is 12(Tbc+Tcb)−iδbc−13δbc(Tdd−3i)=12(Tbc+Tcb)−13δbcTdd,\tfrac12(T_{bc}+T_{cb})-i\delta_{bc}-\tfrac13\delta_{bc}(T_{dd}-3i) =\tfrac12(T_{bc}+T_{cb})-\tfrac13\delta_{bc}T_{dd}\text{,}21​(Tbc​+Tcb​)−iδbc​−31​δbc​(Tdd​−3i)=21​(Tbc​+Tcb​)−31​δbc​Tdd​, the same as before.
